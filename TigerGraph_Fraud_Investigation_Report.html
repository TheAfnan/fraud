1. Executive Summary & Architectural Vision

The objective of this project is to build an analyst-grade Financial Crime Investigation Workstation for the HHGOA Hackathon, driven by an autonomous multi-step Agentic Investigation Loop and backed by an authoritative TigerGraph graph database.

Traditional fraud systems rely on siloed rule engines or isolated black-box tabular classifiers that flag suspicious events without investigating the relational context. This project implements an intelligent, graph-native workflow that mirrors a senior Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) analyst:

  • Case Initiation & Trigger Evaluation: Ingesting transaction alerts (e.g. out-of-region card present, micro-authorizations, new device login).
  • Graph Relational Traversal: Traversing multi-hop connections across cards, customers, devices, proxies, email domains, and billing regions in live TigerGraph.
  • Deterministic Policy Compliance (R1–R10): Applying explicit banking risk rules with tiered escalation (Automated resolution, Level-1 AML approval, Level-2 Compliance sign-off).
  • GraphRAG Memory Retrieval: Matching target typologies against 5,565 historical closed cases stored directly in TigerGraph.
  • SAR Generation & Writeback: Generating FinCEN-compliant Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) and recording the dynamic InvestigationCase back into TigerGraph graph memory.
Authoritative Architectural Rule
TigerGraph is the sole authoritative backend. The local SQLite/NetworkX engine is strictly an offline development fallback and never masquerades as TigerGraph. A persistent health status badge (TIGERGRAPH_LIVE vs LOCAL_FALLBACK) is maintained at all times.

5. Pre-Compiled GSQL Queries & Graph Algorithms

Six specialized GSQL queries were authored, verified, and compiled into native C++ binaries on the live cluster:

  • card_window(VERTEX<Card> target_card, INT window_hours): Traverses card transactions to measure financial velocity, micro-authorization count (<$5.00 online test charges), and total exposure.
  • device_neighbors(VERTEX<DeviceProfile> target_device): Traverses hardware fingerprints across multiple transactions and cards to identify multi-card syndicates and known fraudulent devices.
  • cluster_analysis(VERTEX<Customer> target_customer, STRING target_region): Analyzes historical geographic distributions of a customer's cards to distinguish routine travel from genuine out-of-region anomalies (Rules R2 & R3).
  • find_similar_cases(STRING target_pattern, DOUBLE min_exposure, DOUBLE max_exposure, INT max_results): Case-memory retrieval query filtering by typology and exposure range.
  • detect_fraud_syndicates(INT min_cards_per_cluster): Weakly Connected Components graph algorithm detecting clusters of cards interconnected by shared devices or emails.
  • write_case_to_graph(...): Dynamic agent writeback query that instantiates an InvestigationCase vertex, creates TARGET_CARD and INVESTIGATES edges, and records final findings in TigerGraph.

  • Transactions & Topology: 1,982 transactions for benchmark cards, complete with MADE, BILLED_IN, PURCHASER_EMAIL, and FROM_DEVICE edges.
  • Hardware & Geography: Distinct DeviceProfile, BillingRegion, and EmailDomain vertices.
  • Historical Memory: 2,000 ClosedCase vertices with ON_CARD edges.
  • Total Ingested Records: Over 10,000 vertices and edges.
  • Live Query Verification: Running card_window for card C12382-K1 on live TigerGraph traversed and returned 422 transactions and $48,783.68 exposure in milliseconds.
